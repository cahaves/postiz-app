Current primary platform guidance provides guardrails, with separate dates:

- Instagram's official [@creators announcement](https://www.instagram.com/creators/p/DSalTolEbvf/) has a DOM timestamp of 18 December 2025, 18:46 UTC. It announces up to five hashtags per Reel or post. I read it live on 20 October 2026. Meta's undated [IG User Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media), retrieved through Context7 the same day, still describes captions as up to 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags, and 20 mentions. This is a documentation mismatch. Generate at most five relevant hashtags as a conservative product policy, permit fewer or none, and validate publishing through the actual connector. Do not claim verified universal API rejection at the sixth hashtag.
- Meta's [Threads publishing reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/threads/posts), retrieved through Context7 on 20 October 2026, distinguishes normal 500-character text posts from [text attachments](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/threads/create-posts/text-attachments) of up to 10,000 characters. Meta announced the latter on [4 September 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/attach-text-threads-posts-share-longer-perspectives/), with the page updated 22 October 2025. These are publishing capabilities, not ideal writing lengths. Postiz must actually support the field before offering it. Topic tags, text attachments, reply targets, and alt text are represented separately in [Meta's public Threads API collection](https://www.postman.com/meta/threads/documentation/dht3nzz/threads-api), checked 20 October 2026.
- Meta's [Threads educational insights](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/find-your-community-with-new-threads-educational-insights/), published 21 October 2024 and updated 26 June 2025, reports that replies account for almost half of views and that top creators publish original material for Threads. It also offers frequency, weekend, humor, and media-with-text suggestions. Treat those as platform-wide observed patterns and starting experiments, not mandatory schedules, humor, topics, or engagement guarantees for Cahaves.
- Meta's [Threads topics and insights page](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/03/new-threads-features-more-personalized-experience-you-control/), updated 30 October 2025, reports higher views generally for topic-tagged posts and describes account insights. It publishes no causal estimate or account-specific guarantee. Suggest a relevant topic when the connector supports it, never unrelated trending tags or a mandatory topic on every response.
- Instagram's [Best Practices announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/), dated 1 October 2024, explicitly combines general and personalized account guidance. Prefer account evidence over a fixed universal length, posting time, or frequency.
- Meta's [alt-text explanation](https://about.fb.com/news/2021/01/using-ai-to-improve-photo-descriptions-for-blind-and-visually-impaired-people/), published 19 January 2021 and updated 2 November 2021, prioritizes accurate, useful image descriptions for screen-reader users. Generate alt text from supplied media when supported; do not invent unseen content or use the field as a keyword list. Keep accessibility text distinct from promotional captions.

Independent corroboration is observational. Buffer's [5 March 2026 report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/) reports associations of roughly 42% higher engagement on Threads and 21% on Instagram when accounts reply, using within-account analyses. Its reply study covers nearly two million posts; it represents Buffer users and permits reverse causation. The report's datasets and periods differ by analysis, with most recent data described as through 3 December 2025. It does not test English wording styles, AI-written replies, fixed reply quotas, or Cahaves. This supports making useful replies easy, not promising a numerical lift.

Provisional system-instruction baseline, supported as design hypotheses and editorial choices:

1. Use the selected language and approved brand examples. For English, start with clear conversational wording and natural contractions. Preserve the requested register. Do not infer slang, US spelling, British spelling, humor, profanity, or cultural references from language alone.
2. When the brief contains a real customer problem, describe it concretely and connect it to a verified product action or result. A problem-led caption is one option, not a required template. Do not invent a customer, metric, testimonial, roadmap date, or product capability to make it persuasive.
3. Adapt the same message to the post format. Let an Instagram caption add useful context to the media. Let a Threads post communicate one coherent point, with enough context to start or join a conversation. Keep text as short as the idea permits without removing necessary facts. Runtime limits are hard constraints; desired length is an editable preference.
4. Use a CTA only when the brief has a real next step. A resource or walkthrough invitation can be a soft offer. Do not append a question, product mention, sales pitch, or comment-keyword promise to every post. Offer comment-to-DM wording only when that delivery mechanism is actually configured.
5. Read the root post, target comment, parent chain, previous brand replies, and available product facts before drafting a reply. Answer questions directly. Acknowledge praise specifically. Explain limitations candidly. Ask a follow-up only when the answer is needed. Never invent a named staff signature.
6. Prefer original wording and supported details. Use outside posts to identify approaches, not to copy phrasing or impersonate their author. Keep helpful links, language choice, emoji density, hashtags, topics, and signatures under brand control.

Editable settings should include English locale, formal/casual register, first-person identity, audience, technical vocabulary, slang and code-switching policy, humor, emoji preference, CTA purpose and frequency, desired length by platform and format, hashtag/topic policy, approved claims and links, reply intents, escalation rules, and staff-signature policy. Store good/bad approved examples separately for original posts, comments, support replies, and each language. Scheduling experiments belong in planning settings rather than prose instructions.

Do not encode universal claims such as a perfect caption length, a guaranteed visible first 125 characters, a best posting hour, a required number of weekly posts, a mandatory question, an always-use-humor rule, a required hashtag count, or a promised engagement gain. None is established by this sample. I'm unsure whether any observed style will outperform another for Cahaves. Confidence: 60% on that transfer because brand, audience, distribution, and objectives differ. Approved Cahaves examples and account-level experiments measuring useful replies or business actions would resolve that uncertainty.

Confidence: 94% that the proposed baseline is a reasonable editable starting point; it is a design recommendation, not a measured performance prediction.
