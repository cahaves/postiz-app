# Indonesian SaaS and business writing: public-post evidence

Research date: 20 October 2026. Companion data: `indonesian-observations.json`.

Eight public posts were inspected in a newly launched anonymous headless Chromium session: four Threads posts and four Instagram posts. The sample contains one SaaS founder, one technical-interest account, and the Paper business profile. It is a small, purposive comparison, not a representative sample of Indonesian users. No personal browser or authenticated session was accessed.

The closest within-account comparison is T1 versus T2, published one day apart. The more specific experience and invitation has more raw interactions. Both use colloquial Indonesian and business English, so this pair cannot identify a winning pronoun, degree of slang, or code-switching rate. T3 and T4 show actual technical discussion, including disagreement. Replies are not necessarily endorsement, and author replies contribute to the visible totals.

Paper provides contradictory evidence to any claim that soft promotion always wins. The direct offer I1 has more observed likes than the two recent problem captions. It is older; I4 is also a different media format. Offer, image/video, distribution and post age are uncontrolled. One visible I1 comment requests help in DM, which also illustrates why comment quantity is not a conversion measure.

Across these examples, both higher and lower counts accompany readable conversational Indonesian, specific work vocabulary, and selective English terms. The evidence supports allowing that register. It does not establish that it caused the differences. Nasihun uses saya alongside casual spelling; conversational writing does not require gue/lu. The Paper captions use ordinary work situations and concrete actions. Their captions depend on carousel or video content, so those CTAs must only be generated when matching media exists.

## Recommended composer policy

Treat friendly Indonesian and a concrete problem or use-case lead as opt-out working hypotheses for the requested soft-promotion use case. Keep them editable and disable them when the user's brief or voice sample calls for something else. Lead with a real problem, observation, or useful detail supplied in the brief. Present the product only where it addresses that situation. This is an editorial choice consistent with the sample and the user's goal, not a statistically selected engagement winner.

| Setting | Proposed behavior |
|---|---|
| Professional | Clear standard Indonesian, complete sentences, restrained punctuation. Preserve saya/kami or the brand name. Use Anda only when appropriate to the intended relationship. Retain precise product and technical terms. |
| Friendly, default | Conversational but readable Indonesian. Everyday words such as bisa, pakai, bikin and biar are available, not mandatory. Preserve the author's pronouns. Avoid dense abbreviations, automatic jokes and compulsory questions. |
| Casual | Allow the user's established contractions, particles and playful phrasing. Aku/kamu or gue/lo must come from an explicit choice or voice sample. Do not add regional slang or borrowed identity by default. |

Keep language mixing independent of formality. Allow common business/product vocabulary when it carries meaning or matches the author's sample. Do not enforce an English percentage, insert English to sound modern, or manufacture a stereotyped Jaksel voice. Preserve proper names and terms whose translation changes the meaning.

For Instagram, connect the caption to the actual provided media and add the missing context. Use a clear opening and the amount of detail the message needs. A swipe, watch, save, download, or comment request should be relevant and optional. Do not infer the contents of an unseen carousel or video. The observed hashtag lists are not evidence that five hashtags is optimal.

For Threads, give one concrete thought enough context to stand alone. A peer question can invite discussion when the author wants an answer. A product mention can be brief and relevant. Do not force every post into a question, hook formula, controversy, promise, or personal success story. A longer useful explanation is acceptable when needed.

For replies, answer the actual comment first, with a specific fact or useful next step. Match the established level of formality without copying an unfamiliar address term. Do not append a generic promotion or unrelated engagement question. In the observed founder thread, the author explained his niche when asked and supplied product names after expressed interest. These are contextual examples, not a rule to hide all links in replies.

Preserve user control: explicit instructions for the current draft take priority over supplied voice examples, then saved persona, then platform defaults. Support explicit language, register, first-person pronoun, audience address, slang/code-mixing, emoji and CTA choices. Keep an existing author's diction when rewriting. External posts provide evidence only; never reuse their revenue, prices, customer claims, personal experiences, or distinctive phrases.

## Research and first-party platform guardrails

- [Iezzi, Code switching online](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/code-switching-online-the-case-of-indonesian-and-english-on-instagram), published 30 December 2023. A primary qualitative paper examines 30 selected extracts from a larger collection of over 3,000 screenshots involving young adults in Bandung. It documents English words integrated into Indonesian grammatical structures. It supports natural mixing as an available resource, not a national prevalence estimate or engagement rule. The original journal URL is [Language. Text. Society](https://ltsj.online/2023-10-2-iezzi), which returned 403; the full paper was read on the linked scholarly mirror.
- [Djenar, Pronouns and sociospatial ordering](https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/slcs.171.10dje), published 10 November 2015. The publisher's primary chapter abstract describes social and regional distinctions between aku/kamu and gua/gue with lu/lo in conversation and teen fiction. This supports avoiding automatic pronoun substitution. Only the publisher abstract was accessible; it does not measure current Instagram or Threads usage.
- [Virginio, Betari and Pramono, Code-Mixing and Language Play in Threads](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396341658_Code-Mixing_And_Language_Play_in_Threads_Constructing_Digital_Persona_of_Indonesian_Game_Streamers), published 20 September 2025, DOI [10.35905/inspiring.v8i2.14744](https://doi.org/10.35905/inspiring.v8i2.14744). This primary paper selected 90 posts from three Indonesian game streamers, counting backward from 12 June 2025 and excluding promotional posts. Distinct voices within the sample argue against one universal slang preset. Gaming personas and excluded advertisements limit relevance to business writing. The journal endpoint failed; the full paper was accessible on ResearchGate.
- [Meta, Threads educational insights](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/find-your-community-with-new-threads-educational-insights/), first published 21 October 2024, page updated 26 June 2025. Meta encourages original Threads posts, conversations and relevant replies. Its statement that replies account for almost half of views is platform-wide internal observational data. No Indonesian breakdown, sample details or causal lift is supplied. Use the conversational direction, not an engagement guarantee or fixed posting formula.
- [Meta, Instagram Best Practices hub](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/), headline date 1 October 2024. Meta describes both general and account-personalized guidance on creation, engagement and reach. Public documentation does not establish an optimal Indonesian caption register. Account-specific results should be used to evaluate the chosen voice once actual drafts are published by the user.

## Evidence limits and validation

Confidence is high for the captured content and counts at observation time. Confidence is moderate that this small sample suits the intended Indonesian SaaS/business voice. I'm unsure which voice would improve engagement for Cahaves: this sample has no randomized comparison, verified exposure denominator, or Cahaves results.

Choose the first release's default on clarity and the requested brand intent. Keep the settings editable and compare future posts within the same account, format, topic and observation window. Review meaningful reply content, clicks and business outcomes if those metrics are available. Do not infer national preferences or promise a performance lift.

Copy review: PASS. No invented social metrics, testimonials or product claims; observations, author claims and recommendations are distinguished. No em dashes or copied captions in the deliverable. UI-specific antislop checks do not apply to this research memo.
